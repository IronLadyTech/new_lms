Iron Lady LMS

Session reminders: how they are sent, and how we know they arrived.

Written for anyone who needs to understand the flow — no technical background assumed. The short version: the LMS decides who needs reminding, WhatsApp delivers it, and WhatsApp tells us back whether it arrived and whether it was read.

The whole flow, in one picture

What gets tracked, and where it comes from

Five things are recorded for every reminder. The first four arrive on their own; only the last needs anybody to do anything.

What we know Where it comes from What it tells you
Sent The LMS, when it hands the message over We tried. If this is missing, the learner has no number or has not agreed to be contacted.
Delivered WhatsApp, automatically It reached her phone. If this never arrives, the number is wrong or the phone has been off for days.
Read WhatsApp, automatically She opened it. The blue ticks, reported back to us.
Replied WhatsApp, if she answers A reply opens a 24-hour window in which CX can talk to her freely, at no per-message cost.
Attended / submitted The LMS already knows this The outcome the reminder existed for.

Why this is better than a tick box. A tick box records that somebody pressed send. It cannot tell you the number was wrong, the phone was off, or the message was never opened. The messaging platform reports all three without anyone being asked to keep a list up to date — and a record nobody has to maintain is the only kind that stays true.

The number that actually matters

Delivery is not the goal — attendance and submitted work are. Recording all five steps lets you see exactly where a cohort falls away, and each drop has a different fix.

Reading the funnel — what each drop actually means

Where they fall away What it means Who fixes it
Could not be contacted
Data		 Nothing was sent to them at all. No phone number saved, or one written wrongly — missing the country code, spaces in it, a digit short — or no agreement on file to contact them.

The reason this matters more than the number suggests: these learners are invisible today. Nobody knows they are not getting reminders. They fall behind quietly, and everyone assumes they lost interest when in truth they were never contacted. 		CX, from a list. An afternoon's phoning, not an engineering job.
Delivered but not read
Wording or timing		 It reached her phone and she chose not to open it. Nothing broke — she saw the notification and decided it was not worth a tap.

That points at what it said or when it arrived: sent at an hour nobody looks, or written like a mass broadcast so it gets ignored like one, or saying nothing that applies to her in particular. “Reminder: session tomorrow” gets ignored. “Priya — Session 3 tomorrow 9am, your superpowers list is still pending” gets opened. Same cost, different result. 		Whoever writes the messages. Wording and timing, nothing technical.
Read, attended, still did not submit
The task		 The reminder worked perfectly. She got it, read it, and came to the session. The work still did not arrive.

So sending her more reminders is pointless — and this is the trap, because “chase them harder” is the obvious response and it is the one thing that cannot help. Something about the task itself is in the way: unclear what is wanted, longer than the time she has, or she does not know how to begin. 		The programme team. A worked example, a clearer template, or CX ringing three of them to ask what is stopping them.

Why separating them is the whole point. Without the funnel, all anyone can say is “the reminders are not working”, and the instinct is to send more. More messages would help the first group slightly, irritate the second, and do nothing whatsoever for the third.

The funnel tells you which of the three you have — so the effort goes where it changes something.

When reminders would go out

Your schedule pairs every session with something to hand in, so a reminder can carry both. Three moments do most of the work:

When Who gets it Roughly what it says
Evening before Everyone in the batch “Tomorrow 9:00am — Session 3, Capability Matrix. Bring your top three superpowers.”
One hour before Everyone in the batch A short nudge with the joining link.
Two hours after Only those who did not attend or have not submitted The recording, and what is still outstanding.

The third is where the value is, and it is the one only the LMS can send — it is the only system that knows who was missing and what is still owed.

How personal can a message be?

More personal than most people expect, with one rule that shapes everything: outside a conversation, the sentence structure is fixed and the details slot in.

Meta approves a wording once, with blanks in it. You then fill the blanks per learner. So this is one approved template:

“Hi {name} — {session} is tomorrow at {time}. Your {deliverable} is still outstanding. Reply DONE once it is uploaded.”

→ “Hi Priya — Session 3, Capability Matrix is tomorrow at 9:00am. Your top three superpowers list is still outstanding. Reply DONE once it is uploaded.”

Personalising by Possible? Notes
Her name Yes Already on her record.
Which session, date, time, joining link Yes The LMS holds the schedule.
What she personally still owes Yes The strongest one available, and the only system that knows it is the LMS.
Her profession or seniority If recorded Comes through from Zoho for learners who arrived that way. Worth checking how many actually have it before designing messages around it.
Her stated goal or pain point Only if asked Not held anywhere today. One question at enrolment — “what do you most want from this programme?” — makes every later message sharper.
A freely written message, unique to her Only after she replies See below — this is the rule worth designing around.

The rule that shapes everything: the 24-hour window

You may not send freely written messages to someone out of the blue — only approved wordings with the blanks filled in. But the moment she replies, a 24-hour window opens in which CX can write anything, as often as needed, at no per-message cost.

So design the first message to earn a reply. Ending with “Reply DONE when it is uploaded” is not a courtesy — it converts a broadcast into a conversation, and a conversation is where real coaching can happen. It also tells you she is still with you, which no delivery receipt can.

Ideas for keeping them engaged

Your programme runs from June to January. Over seven months the risk is not that people refuse — it is that they drift. These use what the LMS already knows.

Idea Why it works Effort
Ask for a one-word reply “Reply DONE when uploaded.” Opens the free conversation window, and a silent learner becomes visible before she is three sessions behind. Wording only
Say what everyone else has done “34 of 100 have submitted so far.” People act on what their peers are doing far more than on instructions. The LMS already counts this. Small
Use her streak The product already tracks it. “You have submitted six sessions running” is worth more than any encouragement written by us, because it is hers. Small
Only chase the people who need it The message two hours after a session should go only to those who missed it or still owe the work. Reminding people who already finished is how a channel gets muted. Already possible
Escalate rather than repeat One missed: automatic reminder. Two: a written message from a real person. Three: a phone call. Repeating the same automated nudge teaches people to ignore it. Process, mostly
Ask the ones who stall what is stopping them For the third group in the funnel, one question — “what is getting in the way?” — will teach you more than another reminder ever will, and answers arrive in the free window. CX time

One caution. These are reasoned from your schedule and what the product already records, not from published research into what works for programmes like yours. If you want that grounding before committing, it is worth doing properly rather than assuming — and the first cohort's own funnel will be better evidence than any benchmark.
